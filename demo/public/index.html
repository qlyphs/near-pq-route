Three steps, all on chain.

Your contract keeps running, keeps its balances and can still be upgraded. Only who controls it changes.

01

Hand over

Your contract gives its admin powers to the Guardian, a contract with no keys at all. Then its own keys are deleted.

02

Approve

A mint or an upgrade needs two of three signers to approve the exact operation with ML-DSA-87, the NIST post-quantum signature.

03

Wait, then execute

An on-chain delay leaves time to cancel. After it, anyone can execute. No key can skip the queue.

For NEAR protocols.

Guardian protects the admin path of a contract that adopts it. Your users and your code stay where they are.

  • What you addMint and upgrade methods that accept only the Guardian, as in the reference token. Other protocols need the same kind of compatible methods; that integration is future work.
  • What you keepYour contract, its state and balances, and the ability to upgrade it.
  • What you getAfter the cutover, no classical key can mint or upgrade the token, and every mint or upgrade proposal is public during its delay.
  • What it does not coverUsers' own keys and NEAR consensus. It is an unaudited testnet prototype today.
